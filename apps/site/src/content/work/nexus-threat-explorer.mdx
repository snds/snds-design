Proofpoint wanted to broaden its suite with a comprehensive, elegant tool that
visualized the structure of an attack across a customer's infrastructure. Nexus
Threat Explorer (NTX) was designed to work alongside Targeted Attack Protection
(TAP) — and it had to ship fast.

## The problem

Visualizing threat propagation across a network is hard, and there were few
public examples to learn from. Attacks involve dense, branching relationships
that demanded real ideation to make legible. Meanwhile the foundational product,
TAP, had no scalable, reusable component layer to build on — and we had three to
six months to stand up a working prototype under startup-like constraints.

> **The challenge:** create a new product out of whole cloth, with no established
> set of components or patterns to draw from.

## Approach — find the source

Engineering audited the TAP codebase and found accessibility and scalability
issues that ruled out reuse. Rather than build a system from zero on an
impossible timeline, we evaluated several — IBM Carbon, the U.S. Web Design
System — and chose **Material Design 2.0**: accessible, usable out of the box,
and capable of being both data-rich and approachable.

Early designs paired fixed node diagrams with generalized insights. Customer
interviews pushed us to think bigger — _"go big or go home."_ A granted extra
week of ideation produced a high-fidelity prototype that landed hard with
customers, who started asking _when_ they could have it. Over the following year
we layered in Related IOCs, Compromised Users, and a Saved Graph Dashboard,
validating each with beta participants.

## Outcome

We ideated and shipped a product customers were vocal about wanting — to first
alpha in roughly three months. Beyond NTX, the Material implementation became a
proof point that nudged other business units to explore the same path.
